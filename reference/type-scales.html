    Garden guide

    Plant a parkway garden

    The strip between the sidewalk and the curb takes full sun, road salt and foot traffic. A short list of native perennials handles all three, and feeds the bees on your block.

    Before you dig

    Call 811 a few days ahead so buried lines are marked. Then check the city’s rules for parkway plantings before you choose plants. Height limits near corners and driveways keep sightlines clear for drivers and for kids on bikes.

    Read the light

    Watch the strip on a clear day: in the morning, at noon and in the late afternoon. A parkway in the open gets sun from mid-morning on. One under a street tree may get two hours. Choose plants for the hours you count.

    Soil

    Parkway soil is often compacted fill. Loosen the top 8–12in and work in 2in of leaf compost. Skip it and the roots stay shallow and dry out in August. Overdo the compost and the bed settles below the curb by the second year.

    Four plants that take a parkway
    • Little bluestem (Schizachyrium scoparium), 2–3ft, copper in fall
    • Prairie dropseed (Sporobolus heterolepis), 2ft, fine-leaved mounds
    • Pale purple coneflower (Echinacea pallida), 3ft, flowers in early summer
    • Wild strawberry (Fragaria virginiana), 6in, a groundcover for the edges

    Spacing for a 6ft parkway: 12–18in on center, grasses toward the middle.

    Plant in early fall

    Early fall, once the first street trees start to color. The roots keep growing while the tops slow down, and the plants come up strong in spring. Water twice a week for the first month.
